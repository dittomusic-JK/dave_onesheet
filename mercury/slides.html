The Boy Who Played The Harp is Dave's most introspective, spiritually driven, and politically charged album yet. Rooted in concept-driven storytelling, it confronts guilt, shame, hypocrisy, repentance, and the burden of accountability that comes with newfound influence.

The title track and album centerpiece examines exploitation in Africa — conflict in the Democratic Republic of Congo, blood diamonds, corrupt leadership, and the ongoing plunder of the continent's resources. It tackles social injustices including the occupation of Palestine, the genocide in Gaza, and the fear of speaking out. Dave also exposes broader hypocrisies: wealthy elites enjoying luxury amid widespread suffering, the gap between performative activism and genuine action, plus the legacies of slavery, colonialism, and selective outrage. On "Fairchild" (feat. Nicole Blakk), he powerfully addresses violence against women and rape culture, critiquing all men while confronting male entitlement, sexual harassment, and complicity.

Throughout, Dave draws heavily on biblical imagery, positioning himself as a modern-day David. The harp becomes a metaphor for music's power to soothe torment and serve a higher divine purpose.

Across these themes, he questions society and what we must do to improve it, while relentlessly turning the mirror on himself. The title track's central question, "What would I do in the next generation?", delivers a sobering answer: he already knows, because it depends on what he does right now, today.

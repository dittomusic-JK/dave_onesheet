The Boy Who Played The Harp is Dave’s most introspective, spiritually driven, and politically charged album to date. Rooted in deep respect for concept and storytelling, it confronts guilt, shame, hypocrisy, and repentance, while grappling with the weight of accountability after gaining influence.

At its core, the title track and album centrepiece unpacks exploitation in Africa — from conflict in the Democratic Republic of Congo and blood diamonds, to corrupt leadership and the continued rape and pillage of the continent’s resources — social injustices, explicitly the occupation of Palestine, the genocide in Gaza, and the fear of speaking out. It also exposes broader hypocrisies: wealthy elites (including Dave himself) indulging in luxury while ignoring suffering, the gap between performative activism and real action, and the legacies of slavery, colonialism, and selective outrage. He also tackles violence against women and rape culture, most powerfully on “Fairchild” (feat. Nicole Blakk), where he seeks to help women by critiquing men, and by extension, his own past behaviour, confronting male entitlement, sexual harassment and how men can be complicit.

Heavily biblical, Dave casts himself as a modern-day David, using the image of the harp as a metaphor for his music’s power to soothe torment and fulfil a greater divine purpose.

Across all these overarching themes that thread through the album, Dave questions society and what we as a people can do to improve it, change it, and move it forward, all while turning the mirror on himself. Ultimately, the title track poses the central question of the album: “What would I do in the next generation?”; before delivering the sobering answer: he already knows, because it depends on what he is doing right now, in this moment, today.
